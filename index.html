The problem

A silent epidemic hiding in plain sight

Fatty liver disease is the fastest-growing cause of liver transplantation worldwide. The evidence sits inside scans that are already being taken — it's just not being read.

🔬

A direct physical measurement

As fat accumulates in the liver, CT attenuation drops in a near-linear, predictable way. Marrowen reads this signal directly — no learned proxy, no guesswork.

📋

Opportunistic, not additional

Every routine non-contrast abdominal CT that passes through the liver contains enough data to grade steatosis. Marrowen works on scans that were already ordered for something else entirely.

⏱

Early enough to reverse

Steatosis caught early responds well to lifestyle intervention and emerging pharmacotherapy. Finding it before it becomes fibrosis or cirrhosis is the entire point.

🔒

No additional patient burden

No extra imaging. No biopsy. No gadolinium. No added cost to the scan already performed. Marrowen screens using what's already there.

The pipeline

Three steps. Under ten seconds.

Marrowen's design is deliberately simple — fewer moving parts means fewer failure points, and a single dominant biomarker that is a direct physical measurement means accuracy doesn't depend on a complex model.

1

Ingest & Filter

The scan is read and checked for contrast enhancement via DICOM metadata. Contrast-enhanced series are excluded automatically before any measurement is taken.

2

Liver Segmentation

The liver and spleen volumes are identified and outlined. Mean Hounsfield Unit attenuation is measured within the liver mask using the original, unmodified scan voxels.

3

Grade & Report

A steatosis grade is assigned. The output is a one-page PDF per patient with the grade, risk score, and an explanation of which features drove the result.

Output

Four grades, one clear recommendation

Each scan produces a grade, a numerical risk score, and an actionable clinical recommendation — written for the radiologist, not the algorithm.
